## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js (user's choice). Workflow set by the user: design the page in Figma first, then build it in Next.js, then test it with Playwright.

## Users

Owners and managing directors of UK businesses with roughly 10 to 250 staff, who can approve spend themselves without a procurement process. Most have tried ChatGPT or a single AI tool, but their day-to-day processes (enquiries, invoicing, customer records, rotas, document chasing) still run on people, inboxes and spreadsheets. They hold back because of cost, skills, data-protection worries, lack of time, doubt about the payoff, and above all not knowing where to start. A meaningful minority tried AI and gave up after a poor implementation.

Secondary audience: accountants, bookkeepers, IT support firms and business advisers, who are the first place small firms ask for technology advice and who refer clients on.

## Product Purpose

Ameliora is a B2B AI automation consultancy. It finds where a business loses time or money to repetitive work, works out which automations are worth building, builds them, and keeps them running. Each piece of work saves the client time, money, or both, measured in the client's own numbers.

Success for the website: a qualified business owner books a free 30-minute discovery call.

## Positioning

Tool-agnostic delivery from people who can build either way: the founders work in both low-code/no-code platforms and pro-code (custom software and AI engineering), so each problem gets whichever is right for that business: a feature the client already pays for, a no-code workflow, or custom code. Most competitors can only do one of these and force every problem into their tool.

Ameliora sells a measured result (hours saved, enquiries answered faster, errors removed), agreed up front and measured before and after, not a technology.

Ameliora uses an internal target niche when it chooses which businesses to approach, but the website does not mention it and presents the firm to UK businesses generally.

## Operating Context

How an engagement runs:

1. Discovery call: free, 30 minutes, to understand the business, the painful manual process and who decides.
2. Paid diagnostic: map 2 to 4 processes, measure hours and error rates, deliver a prioritised roadmap with the expected return of each automation. The fee is credited against a build.
3. Pilot build: one automation, 2 to 4 weeks, one measurable target agreed up front (for example "enquiries answered within 5 minutes").
4. Ongoing care (monthly): hosting, monitoring, fixing breakages when vendors change things, model upgrades, and improvements over time.
5. Expand: the next item off the roadmap.

An alternative way in is a team AI workshop.

How the work is done: built inside the client's own accounts wherever possible; the client owns the accounts, code and workflows; no lock-in; a data processing agreement as standard; a person approves customer-facing AI output until it is proven reliable; independent, with no referral fees from software vendors. Tools used include n8n, Make, Zapier, Power Automate, Copilot Studio, Python and TypeScript, but clients buy outcomes, so tools come second.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Ameliora. No existing logo, colours or typefaces.
- Voice: plain English that talks in outcomes (hours, pounds, response times, errors), not tools or jargon. Mention tools only when they help the reader decide.
- Presence: the site presents Ameliora as an established firm in the company's voice ("we"). It never states or hints at the team's size (no "two people", no founder names or photos, no husband-and-wife framing) for now. Equally, it never invents size: no headcounts, staff profiles, offices or client numbers that are not true. Trust comes from specifics, process, commitments and company details instead.
- Look (30 Oct 2026, after a full redesign round): "serious experts", professional B2B, researched against real AI consultancies (the owner's steer: "more professional for a B2B. look into what other ai consultancy websites look like"). Chosen direction: The Documentary, black-and-white photography of British businesses at work, plain type, white ground, one small orange accent. Standing preference: the familiar, professional end over expressive concepts, but never the generic "AI website" look (glows, gradients, dot grids, abstract 3D). Earlier rejections: expressive concept directions as "portfolio sites"; the white-and-green minimal build as bare, gimmicky (animated job pile, cost calculator) and underselling ("admin").
- Page feel (user's words, 29 Oct 2026): no per-section background colours or "blocky" bands, which read as AI-made; the background is one seamless, continuous ground. Apple-style scroll moments are wanted: a section holds in place while its animation plays, then the page moves on.
- Less is more (user's words, 29 Oct 2026): the text-heavy version was rejected outright ("it has too much text"). The aim is "minimalistic, sleek", with "the concept straight up and obvious": show rather than say, about 200 words on the page, and only one Apple-style pinned section ("just in one section is enough").
- Where the motion goes (user's words, 30 Oct 2026): "make the scroll to animate the how it works part and make the hero just normal animation on load". The hero plays once on load and is never pinned; the one pinned scroll scene is "How it works".
- Not bare, not pigeonholed (user's words, 30 Oct 2026): "the page looks bare and plain, can you add more to it", then, of a section showing four example automations, "i dont like the what we automate section because it pigeon holes us. also i meant something in the background to make the page more full". Fullness comes from the background, not from lists of the work.

## Product Principles

1. Sell the outcome, measured in the client's own numbers, before and after.
2. Right tool for the job: an existing feature first, then no-code, then custom code.
3. Saying yes should feel safe: a small paid first step, the success measure agreed up front, no lock-in, and the client owns everything built.
4. The client's data stays the client's.
5. Be honest about AI: people stay in the loop until the automation has earned trust.
